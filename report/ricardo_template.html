The whole argument, on one page

What the data shows, what I recommend, and where each part is argued. Read this first; the rest is the evidence.

Four findings

  1. The daily film is the bond. 414 public films in 310 days. Before 25 August, the daily vlog carried a 16.4% like rate — above the channel's 15.6% median — on 1,975 views.
  2. The door is the package. None of the 14 most-watched films is titled like a diary entry: seven are idea films, six are object films, one is a collab.
  3. August changed the audience. Median views rose 55% after 25 August, while the like rate fell from 15.6% to 6.0%. Reach arrived; familiarity did not.
  4. The top-right corner is empty. No format combines above-median reach with above-median affinity. The essay is closest: 2,849 views and 14.4% before the shift.

01

The diary is not the problem

He already shoots the film every day. The next step is to cut it — not into something else, but into something a stranger can enter.

The vlog is the relationship. The essay is the door.

Ricardo's channel is built on a rare promise: a film a day, made from a real life. The daily vlog is not a format he chose because it performs. It is the practice. It gives him the footage, the discipline, and the relationship with the people who follow along. In 2026 he published 414 public videos in 310 days — more than one a day on average — and they drew 1.38 million views.

The problem is not the frequency. It is the frame. Most uploads are titled and thumbnailed like diary entries: “café”, “Cores em Movimento”, “287/365”, “VLOG 1019”. A diary entry is an invitation only if you already know the diarist. For a stranger, it is a closed door.

The 14 films that travelled furthest in 2026 did something different. They made a promise before the film began: a claim, a question, a test, a stake. The film inside was still his — warm, first-person, unhurried. The difference was the door.

The opportunity is not to become a different filmmaker. It is to give the daily film a door: a title that promises, a poster that shows a face and an object, a first twenty seconds that starts at the stake. The essay already does most of this. It is the bridge between the diary and the stranger.

What this report is. A reading of the channel's public surface — titles, thumbnails, lengths, views, likes, comments, and 58 transcripts — focused only on 2026. It is a diagnosis of the frame, not a verdict on the work.

What it is not. A Studio report. Impressions, click-through rate, watch time, traffic sources, and returning viewers are not available. Every causal claim here is a hypothesis, clearly marked as one.

02

The year in one look

A spring plateau, a summer dip, a September jump. Then a second story: the like rate halves.

Median views hovered between 1,749 and 2,542 from February through August. January was the outlier at 3,469, powered by the year's biggest film. September and October more than doubled the summer median, at 3,491 and 3,614. November is partial — 12 videos in six days, median 2,762 — and should be read as a running number, not a finished month.

The second panel is the warning. The median like rate was remarkably stable — between 13.1% and 17.1% — for the first eight months. In September it fell to 6.1%, and it stayed near 5–6% in October and November. More views; a smaller share of them liked. In public data, that is what a larger and less familiar audience looks like.

03

The August turn

Reach doubled. Affinity did not. The most useful event of the year is the one that shows what happens when a gear story opens the door.

The break is not July's MacBook film. July's median like rate was still 15.8%. The shift begins with the 25 August “O QUE EU LEVO NA MOCHILA” (5,213 views, 5.9% likes) and the 26 August “Usei o MacBook Air M5 por 30 Dias” (19,771 views, 2.7%). From that point, the channel's median video reached 3,416 views — 55% more than the 2,208 median before — while the median like rate fell from 15.6% to 6.0%, comments from 47 to 26, and the share of videos crossing 5,000 views rose from 7% to 26%.

Two readings are consistent with the public data. First, the gear films brought in a broader audience that does not yet have a relationship with Ricardo: they watch, but they do not like or comment at the old rate. Second, the channel's centre of gravity moved from the daily film toward the product film, and the daily films that followed inherited a different audience mix. Both can be true. Without traffic-source and returning-viewer data, neither can be proven.

What is clear: reach is available. The August wave shows the channel can find strangers. The task is not to keep chasing the wave; it is to convert the strangers the wave brought. A gear film can be the first film someone sees. The next film has to make them stay.

The top 14 films are not all gear. Seven are idea films — manifestos, essays, arguments. Six are object films. One is a collab. Gear is not the only door; it is the most reliable door in the current packaging, because a product in the title and a product in the thumbnail tell a stranger exactly what the film will do.

The risk is that the door becomes the house. A channel that only opens with products will be read as a product channel, and the daily film — the thing that makes the relationship — will look like filler to the new audience. The next move is a bridge, not a pivot.

The honest caveat. The August shift could also reflect a recommendation change, a seasonal effect, or a single video's spillover. The public data cannot separate those from the packaging story. The pattern is consistent with a broader, cooler audience; it does not prove one.

04

The format portfolio

Four ways of making a film. Each buys something different. None yet buys everything.

Before 25 August — to keep the audience shift out of the comparison — the four formats split cleanly. Gear reaches furthest (median 3,696 views) and is liked least (11.0%). The essay reaches 2,849 and holds a 14.4% like rate, close to the channel's 15.6% pre-August median. The daily vlog and the uncategorised diary films, grouped, reach 1,975 with a 16.4% like rate. LEI reaches least (1,397) and is liked most (17.3%).

The upper-right quadrant — more reach and more engagement than the channel median — is empty. The essay is the closest thing to it: it clears the reach line by 29% and sits only 1.2 points below the affinity line. That gap is the creative brief for the next film: an essay with the intimacy of a vlog.

After 25 August, gear's median rose to 8,128 views across six videos, with a 4.1% like rate; essays had a median of 3,342 across eight, with 6.1%. The samples are small, but the direction matches the August story: gear scales reach, not affinity.

Format assignments are keyword-based and approximate. “Daily vlog / other” combines explicit VLOG titles with diary films the classifier could not place; it is a bucket, not a genre.
05

The 14 films that opened a door

The year's most-watched work. Not all gear, not all essays — but all of them make a promise before the film begins.

Seven are idea films — manifestos, essays, arguments. Six are object films — a laptop swap, a camera comparison, a 30-day test. One is a collab. None is titled like a diary entry.

The titles do the same job in different dialects. They make a claim (“PARE DE TENTAR CRIAR CONTEÚDO”, “A VIDA É UM FILME”), ask a question (“e se?”, “DJI Pocket 4 ou Câmera Profissional?”), stage a test (“Usei o MacBook Air M5 por 30 Dias”), or name a stake (“troquei meu MacBook PRO por um Air M5”). A stranger can tell what the film is about before clicking.

The thumbnails repeat the promise in pictures: a face, an object, and often three words. The contact sheet below shows six of them. The pattern is not a formula; it is a habit of showing the stakes.

Length is not the lever. The top 14 have a median length of 16.2 minutes; the bottom 12 of the deep sample, 4.3. But one top film runs 8.7 minutes and another 60.1. What separates them is not duration; it is whether the film has a shape a stranger can enter.

06

What opens a door

Five patterns shared by the films that travelled. They are packaging decisions, not identity decisions.

01

A promise, not a label

A label answers “what is it?” A promise answers “why should I watch?” The bottom titles name a mood or a series position: “café”, “Cores em Movimento”, “287/365”. The top titles name a belief, a question, or a stake.

02

A face and an object

In the top sample, 10 of 12 thumbnails have a prominent face and 8 carry text. In the bottom sample, 5 have a face and 3 carry text. The object gives the face a subject: a MacBook, a camera, a mug. This is a sample observation, not a controlled test.

03

Start at the stake

The strongest films open on the decision, the test, or the question — not on the morning routine. The daily film can still breathe; the first twenty seconds just need to tell a stranger what is at stake.

04

Three acts, not a sequence

A film needs a turn: the reason, the test, the verdict; the before, the day, the after; the belief, the doubt, the proof. The vlog's chronology is the material, not the structure.

05

A door at the end

The sign-off is a relationship ritual; it is not a next step. A door can be a question answered in the next film, a series the viewer can follow, or a promise made in the cold open and paid off at the end. The archive becomes a ladder, not a stream.

07

The films that didn't travel

The bottom 12 of the deep sample are not bad films. They are unopened doors.

Their titles are mood labels (“Cores em Movimento”, “traga-me o horizonte”), place labels (“Caminhos de Outono”), serial numbers (“287/365”, “VLOG 1019”), or gear labels (“vlog com Ray-Ban Meta Gen 2”). Their thumbnails are often landscapes, backs, or atmospheres: beautiful, but without a face or a stake. A stranger has no reason to click; the already-converted need no reason.

The comments show what happens once someone is inside. “Dói saber que eu não terei mais o meu vlog infinito.” The audience mourns the end of the daily vlog. “Ricardo, dei iniciativa de registrar meu dia-dia hoje, por total inspiração dos seus filmes.” The work lands. The threshold is the problem.

These films are also the proof of identity: the channel would be poorer without them. The recommendation is not to stop making them. It is to give the ones that matter a door — and to keep the diary films as the daily floor, not the whole building.

08

The line that must not move

The identity is not the problem. It is the asset. The change is in the frame around the film, not in the film.

“Eu tô nessa para documentar minha vida e não para criar conteúdo.”
“I'm in this to document my life, not to create content.”
“Eu não preciso competir com ninguém.”
“I don't need to compete with anyone.”
“A prioridade é continuar fazendo os filmes que a gente gosta de fazer e não só os filmes para atrair tantas pessoas.”
“The priority is to keep making the films we like to make, not just the films that attract the most people.”

Two phrases worth keeping on the wall. “Eu viro uma câmera.” — I become a camera. “Cena conforto.” — comfort scene. They describe a way of looking, not a content strategy. Any recommendation that requires him to stop looking that way is the wrong recommendation.

The sign-off. “Eu sou Ricardo Franzen, muito obrigado, voltem sempre, voltem amanhã porque amanhã tem mais.” It is a ritual of return. The next cut should not replace it; it should give the viewer one more reason to accept it.

Change

  • The frameTitle, thumbnail, cold open, ending. These are decisions about how a stranger meets the film.
  • The release shapeOne flagship a week, replacing a daily slot. Not adding work; changing which day carries the door.
  • The seriesGive the archive a ladder: essays, object stories with a stake, LEI. Each film points to the next.
  • The measurementUse the public proxies: views vs. rolling median, like rate, comment rate, new commenters.
09

The next cut

Five moves, one month, no change to the daily promise. A flagship replaces a slot; it does not add one.

01

One flagship a week

Replace one daily slot with a 10–20 minute film built around one idea. The other six days stay daily. The 500-video goal is untouched.

02

Write the title before you shoot

A claim, a question, or a stake. If the title is a label — a place, a mood, a number — the film has no door yet. Keep the poetic title as an on-screen chapter card.

03

Shoot the poster

One frame with a face, an object, and three words. The thumbnail is a poster for the film, not a frame grab. It does not need a reaction face; it needs a subject.

04

Open at the stake

The first twenty seconds state the question, the swap, the test, or the fear. After that, the film can breathe at the pace it already has.

05

Build a ladder

Three series: essays on documenting life, object stories with a real stake, and LEI. Each flagship ends on the next question, not only on the sign-off.

11

What this data cannot prove

The limits are part of the finding. Without Studio, the report can describe the surface and propose a hypothesis; it cannot adjudicate it.

No impressions or CTR

We cannot tell whether a video was shown to more people, clicked more often, or both. A view is the end of a funnel we cannot see.

No watch time or retention

We cannot see where viewers leave, whether the 60-minute film holds, or whether the short films are watched to the end.

No traffic sources

The August shift is consistent with a product-led audience wave. It could also be a recommendation change or a seasonal effect. Public data cannot separate them.

No returning viewers

The like-rate collapse suggests a less familiar audience, but it could also reflect a change in viewing context or in the videos themselves.

Cumulative views

Views are a snapshot. Older videos have had more time. The rolling baseline controls for the previous 20 uploads, not for age perfectly.

A 40-film sample

Comments, transcripts, and thumbnail review come from 40 of 414 videos. The sample is deliberately stratified, not random.

Top-sorted comments

1,785 comments, sorted by top, not a representative poll. YouTube may hide spam and low-quality comments; the sample reflects what is visible.

58 transcripts

Only 58 videos have a transcript in the corpus. The 66,965-view film has none. Its reading relies on title, thumbnail, metrics, and comments.

Approximate formats

The classifier is keyword-based. “Other” is a residual, not a genre. Some essays are counted as other, and some gear films are counted as vlogs.

24 members-only videos

They are excluded from the 414. They may contain some of the most engaged audience, and their absence may bias the engagement averages.

12

For Ricardo

What the public numbers say — and what I would do in the next cut. Without changing who you are.

  1. The daily vlog is not the problem. It is the floor. The films that built the most connection in 2026 were the ones that let life in. The comments prove it: people started recording their own day because of your films.
  2. The door is the package. Most videos are titled like a diary: “café”, “Cores em Movimento”, “VLOG 1019”, “287/365”. People who already follow you open them. People who don't keep scrolling.
  3. The 14 films that travelled furthest made a promise before they began: a thesis, a question, a test, a trade. It isn't clickbait; it's clarity. The title says what the film came to do.
  4. The essay is the format that best balances reach and bond. Before 25 August, essays had a median of 2,849 views and 14.4% likes; the daily vlog, 1,975 and 16.4%; gear, 3,696 and 11.0%. The essay reaches further without losing intimacy.
  5. The upper corner of the map is empty. No format combines, at the same time, more reach and more bond than the channel median. That space is the next film: an essay with the intimacy of a vlog.
  6. The August wave proved there is reach. It also showed the cost. The like rate fell from 15.6% to 6.0%, and comments from 47 to 26 per video. The new audience arrived; it doesn't know you yet. The task is to introduce yourself, not to chase the algorithm.
  7. One flagship film a week, in place of one daily video. Keep the 500. Don't add work; change the frame around one day. A 10–20 minute film, one idea, one promise.
  8. Write the title before you shoot. If the title is a label, the film doesn't have a door yet. Keep the poetic title for the opening card.
  9. The thumbnail is a poster: face + object + three words. It doesn't need a reaction face; it needs honesty. The object gives the face a subject.
  10. Open at the stake. In the first 20 seconds, say why this film exists. Then let the day breathe at the pace it already has.
  11. End with a door: a question the next film answers, a series the viewer can follow, a promise the opening made and the ending keeps.
  12. What doesn't change: the first person, the warmth, the sign-off, “come back tomorrow”, the refusal to copy trends, the LEI workshop. The frame changes, not the film.

You already shoot every day. The next cut is giving the film that already exists a door for someone who doesn't know you yet.

Method & sources

Enough detail to reproduce the numbers, and enough caveats to keep them honest.

Corpus

Videos tab of @ricardofranzenart, filtered to 2026. 438 entries; 24 members-only excluded; 414 public videos published 1 January – 6 November 2026. Metadata snapshot: 6 November 2026.

Rolling baseline

ratio = views ÷ median(views of the previous 20 uploads), computed per video at publish time. It controls for channel growth and for the fact that older videos have had more time to accumulate views. Top and bottom lists include only videos at least 14 days old.

Deep sample

40 videos: 12 top rolling outliers, 12 bottom outliers, 8 median performers (one per month), and 8 recent uploads. Used for comments, thumbnails, and transcripts. The sample is stratified by design, not random.

Comments

1,785 comments across 40/40 videos; 789 unique authors. Up to 60 parent comments and 20 replies per video, sorted by top. The top 10 commenters wrote 17.9% of the sample; one regular appeared on 38 of 40 videos. Ricardo replied 191 times.

Transcripts

58 Portuguese auto-generated transcripts, fetched with youtube-transcript-api. Captions are machine-generated and contain errors. The 66,965-view film has no transcript in the corpus.

Thumbnails

40 thumbnails downloaded at the highest available resolution and reviewed on labelled contact sheets. The “face / text / object” counts are a visual review of 24 images (12 top, 12 bottom), not a controlled test.

Format classifier

Keyword priority: LEI → Q&A → gear → essay → travel → photo → VLOG → other. It is a heuristic, not a manual coding of all 414 videos. “Other” absorbs diary films that do not match a keyword.

What is public

Views, likes, comments, titles, descriptions, durations, publish dates, thumbnails, and visible comment text. No impressions, CTR, watch time, traffic sources, returning viewers, or subscriber sources.
